# Stage 8: User Content

From the launch readiness guide at https://ducktate.com/learn/launch-readiness/ (checked 2026-10-09). Not legal advice. Each item is tagged [Must do], [Should do] or [If it applies] and lists its sources.

**In a Lovable project:** Anything users upload to storage buckets or post into tables that other users can read counts as hosted user content.

## Checklist

- [ ] Write down whether your app hosts user content, and which future feature would change that answer. [Must do] Sources: 17 U.S.C. 512 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512), TAKE IT DOWN Act text (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm)
- [ ] If users can upload or post, register a DMCA designated agent online with the Copyright Office for $6. [If it applies] Sources: DMCA Designated Agent Directory (https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/), Copyright Office fees (https://www.copyright.gov/about/fees.html)
- [ ] Put the same agent contact details on a copyright page on your website, linked from your terms. [If it applies] Sources: 17 U.S.C. 512 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512), Copyright Office FAQ (https://www.copyright.gov/rulemaking/onlinesp/NPR/faq.html)
- [ ] Add calendar reminders to renew the agent designation before it expires three years after you register it. [If it applies] Sources: 37 CFR 201.38 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/201.38), Copyright Office FAQ (https://www.copyright.gov/rulemaking/onlinesp/NPR/faq.html)
- [ ] Write a takedown procedure and a repeat infringer policy, tell users about the policy in your terms, and follow both. [If it applies] Sources: 17 U.S.C. 512 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512)
- [ ] Do not rely on Section 230 for copyright claims; use the DMCA process for those. [If it applies] Sources: 47 U.S.C. 230 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230)
- [ ] If users can post images or video others see, build a removal process for non-consensual intimate images that meets the 48-hour limit. [If it applies] Sources: TAKE IT DOWN Act text (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm), FTC business guidance (https://www.ftc.gov/node/331760)
- [ ] Post a plain-language notice that explains how to request removal, and make the request form work for people without an account. [If it applies] Sources: TAKE IT DOWN Act text (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm), FTC business guidance (https://www.ftc.gov/node/331760)
- [ ] Give every removal request a tracking number and an alert that reaches you on weekends. [If it applies] Sources: FTC business guidance (https://www.ftc.gov/node/331760), TakeItDown.ftc.gov (https://takeitdown.ftc.gov)
- [ ] If you have EU users, publish one electronic point of contact and terms that explain your content rules and moderation. [If it applies] Sources: DSA Article 12 (mirror) (https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_12.html), DSA Article 14 (mirror) (https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_14.html)
- [ ] If you host content for EU users, add an electronic notice-and-action form that confirms receipt and tells the reporter your decision. [If it applies] Sources: DSA Article 16 (mirror) (https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_16.html)
- [ ] If you have no EU base but offer your service in the EU, appoint a legal representative in an EU country. [If it applies] Sources: DSA Article 13 (mirror) (https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_13.html), European Commission DSA page (https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act)
