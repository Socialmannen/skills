# Stage 3: Privacy Policy, Terms and Store Paperwork

From the launch readiness guide at https://ducktate.com/learn/launch-readiness/ (checked 2026-10-09). Not legal advice. Each item is tagged [Must do], [Should do] or [If it applies] and lists its sources.

**In a Lovable project:** Build the data inventory from the real project: tables and columns in Lovable Cloud, auth providers, storage buckets, connectors and secrets in use, AI features, analytics scripts and email sending.

## Checklist

- [ ] Write a data inventory from your real code: every field, SDK, analytics tool, AI provider and log, where each one sends data, and for how long you keep it. [Must do] Sources: Apple App Review Guidelines 5.1.1 (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), GDPR Art. 13 (https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)
- [ ] Start from a template, then edit it to match what your app really does. [Should do] Sources: General Legal templates (CC0) (https://github.com/General-Legal/legal-templates), Basecamp policies (https://github.com/basecamp/policies)
- [ ] Make sure your privacy policy covers the six CalOPPA items: PII and third-party categories, how a user can review and request changes to their own PII if you offer that, how you announce changes, the effective date, your Do Not Track answer, and whether third parties may track users over time and across sites. [Must do] Sources: Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code Sec. 22575 (https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_22575)
- [ ] If people in the EU can use your app, add the GDPR Article 13 items: who you are, purposes and legal basis, recipients, transfers, retention, rights, and the right to complain. [If it applies] Sources: GDPR Art. 13 (https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/), GDPR Art. 3 (https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/)
- [ ] Read the finished policy against your inventory and delete or fix every promise the app does not keep. [Must do] Sources: FTC on OkCupid (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-takes-action-against-match-okcupid-deceiving-users-sharing-personal-data-third-party), Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code Sec. 22576 (https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_22576)
- [ ] Post the privacy policy as a public web page, not a PDF, and link it from your app and your store listing. [Must do] Sources: Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code Sec. 22575 (https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_22575), Google Play User Data policy (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- [ ] If you use Google sign-in or list on Google Play, host the policy on your own domain and link it from the OAuth consent screen. [If it applies] Sources: Google OAuth verification (https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321), Google Play User Data policy (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- [ ] Show your terms at sign-up with an unticked "I agree" box or a button that says it means agreement, with the terms linked next to it, and save who agreed to which version. [Should do] Sources: Nguyen v. Barnes and Noble (https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2014/08/18/12-56628.pdf), Berman v. Freedom Financial (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6458861/daniel-berman-v-freedom-financial-network-llc/)
- [ ] Put a cookie notice on your site that lists each cookie or similar storage and why, and get consent first for anything not strictly necessary if you have EU visitors. [If it applies] Sources: ePrivacy Directive Art. 5 (https://legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2002/58/article/5)
- [ ] If users can create an account, let them delete it inside the app, and on Google Play also give a web link for deletion. [If it applies] Sources: Apple account deletion (https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/), Google Play account deletion (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
- [ ] Fill in Apple's App Privacy answers and Google Play's Data safety form, including your SDKs, and redo them when your data use changes. [If it applies] Sources: Apple App Privacy details (https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/manage-app-privacy), Google Play Data safety (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- [ ] If you offer Google sign-in, put a homepage that describes the app on your own verified domain and get OAuth verification, because an unverified app is capped at 100 new users in total. [If it applies] Sources: Google OAuth verification (https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321), Google unverified apps (https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/7454865)
- [ ] If you take payments with Stripe, show what you sell, the currency, contact details beyond a form, your refund and cancellation policy, your privacy policy and your business address. [If it applies] Sources: Stripe website checklist (https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/checklist/website)
- [ ] Update the policy and its effective date every time you add a vendor, SDK, analytics tool or AI provider. [Must do] Sources: Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code Sec. 22575 (https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_22575)
- [ ] Check once a year whether you now pass the CCPA tests or another state's privacy law threshold. [Should do] Sources: CPPA thresholds (https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html), IAPP state tracker (https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-legislation-tracker/)
